Studying was a tiring task. The 400 meaningless shlokas were the worst part. Ten days before, I had passed both the Prout and spiritual philosophy examinations, but I had yet to fully memorize the shlokas. Every time I looked at them, my head spun. Sometimes I thought that I forgot more than I remembered.

Difficult as it was, the studying had its own merit—to discipline the mind even while feeling exhaustion and discomfort.

I really had no idea how I managed but, by cramming and by the Lord’s grace, I learned enough shlokas to pass the exam. There were many I did not know, but by “chance” the examiner quizzed me mostly on the shlokas I had learned. Of course I would very soon forget them, but it did not matter—my concentration had been improved radically by the exercise.

The second phase of my training was now completed. I would go to Patna for the third phase. Perhaps it was just in time—for my health.

**Pandemonium with a purpose**

Though Ananda Marga’s permanent global office is in Ananda Nagar, the global camp office was then in Patna—it was here that Baba lay in a poorly ventilated, inferior jail cell. Patna was a half-day’s bus ride away from the Nepal border. It was an ordinary congested Indian city filled with poverty, filth, and hidden mysticism.

How can I describe our global office? It was certainly not like the stereotyped image of a yoga school or spiritual center—beautiful, calm, immaculately clean, organized, with disciplined class programs, healthy food shared collectively, everything moving according to a fixed schedule, and a staff who reflected only profound inner peace.
